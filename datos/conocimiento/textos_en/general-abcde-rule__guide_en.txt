The ABCDE rule: a quick guide to recognizing melanoma
Source: https://medlineplus.gov/spanish/melanoma.html; https://www.cancer.gov/espanol/tipos/piel/paciente/tratamiento-melanoma-pdq; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/melanoma/symptoms-causes/syc-20374884; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/moles/symptoms-causes/syc-20375200

The ABCDE rule is a simple tool for evaluating a mole or pigmented spot and deciding when to seek medical advice. It does not replace a dermatologist's evaluation, but it helps recognize early warning signs of melanoma.

Each letter stands for one feature. A is for Asymmetry: if you draw a line through the middle, the two halves do not match. B is for Border: the edges are irregular, notched, scalloped or blurred. C is for Color: the color is not uniform and may combine shades of brown, black, red, white, gray or blue. D is for Diameter: usually larger than 6 millimeters, about the size of a pencil eraser, although some melanomas are smaller when found. E is for Evolution: the lesion changes in size, shape, color or elevation, or develops new symptoms such as itching, tenderness or bleeding.

Useful additions: the "ugly duckling" sign (a mole that looks different from the rest), a new pigmented lesion in an adult, and checking hidden areas such as the scalp, soles, between the toes, genital area and under the nails.

Limits: some melanomas, such as nodular or amelanotic ones, do not follow the classic rule. They may be symmetrical, pink and grow quickly, so elevation, firmness and growth are also warning signs.

What to do: if any criterion is present, book a dermatology appointment. Do not try to remove the lesion at home. The diagnosis is confirmed by biopsy. Early detection greatly improves outcomes.
