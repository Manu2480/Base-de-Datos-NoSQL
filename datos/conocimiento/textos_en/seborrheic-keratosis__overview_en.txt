Seborrheic keratosis: benign "stuck-on" skin growth
Source: https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/seborrheic-keratosis/symptoms-causes/syc-20353878; https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000884.htm; https://medlineplus.gov/spanish/melanoma.html

Seborrheic keratosis is a very common benign skin growth, especially after age 40. It is not cancer, not contagious, and rarely becomes malignant. The cause is unknown; it runs in families and increases with age, so it is sometimes called an age wart, although it is not caused by HPV. Lesions appear most often on the trunk, back, chest, face and scalp, and can be single or numerous. A sudden eruption of many lesions (Leser-Trelat sign) is rare and needs evaluation for internal disease.

It looks like a well-defined waxy, rough or warty plaque that seems "stuck on" the skin, ranging from beige to brown or black, from a few millimeters to several centimeters. It is usually painless, though it may itch or be irritated by clothing and may bleed if injured. Because it can be dark, it may be mistaken for melanoma; melanoma is usually asymmetric with irregular borders, multiple colors and change over time. Diagnosis is usually clinical with dermoscopy; a biopsy is done if there is doubt.

No treatment is needed unless it bothers the person, becomes irritated, bleeds or for cosmetic reasons. Options performed by a professional include cryotherapy with liquid nitrogen, curettage, shave removal, laser, or topical 40% hydrogen peroxide for selected lesions. Do not scratch or remove lesions at home. Suspicious lesions should be excised and sent for pathology rather than destroyed.

See a doctor if a lesion grows rapidly, changes color, bleeds without injury, ulcerates, or if many new lesions appear suddenly.
