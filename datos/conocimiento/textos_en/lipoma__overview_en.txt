Lipoma: benign fatty lump, diagnosis and removal
Source: https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/lipoma/symptoms-causes/syc-20374470; https://www.msdmanuals.com/es/hogar (Manual MSD, lipomas)

A lipoma is a benign tumor of fat tissue that grows slowly under the skin. It is the most common soft-tissue lump in adults. It is not cancer and very rarely turns malignant; liposarcoma is a different, uncommon tumor. The cause is unknown, with a hereditary component, and it is most common between ages 40 and 60. Some people have multiple lipomas. Common sites are the neck, shoulders, back, abdomen, arms and thighs, usually 1 to 10 cm.

A lipoma feels soft, doughy and movable under the skin, with well-defined edges and normal skin on top. It is usually painless unless it presses on a nerve, contains blood vessels or sits where there is friction. It differs from an epidermoid cyst (firmer, attached to the skin, with a central punctum, may become infected), a lymph node, and sarcoma (hard, fixed, rapidly growing, deep, usually over 5 cm). Diagnosis is usually clinical; ultrasound or MRI is used if there is doubt, and biopsy if malignancy is suspected.

Most lipomas need no treatment. Removal is considered if the lump hurts, grows, limits movement or for cosmetic reasons. Surgical excision under local anesthesia is the definitive treatment and removes the capsule, which lowers recurrence. Liposuction leaves a smaller scar but may recur more often. No medicine, diet or massage makes lipomas disappear, and they should not be squeezed.

See a doctor if a lump grows quickly, becomes hard, painful or fixed, is larger than 5 cm or lies deep. Tell the surgeon about blood thinners before surgery.
