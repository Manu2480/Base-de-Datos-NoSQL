Sun protection: how to protect your skin from ultraviolet radiation
Source: https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/skin-cancer/symptoms-causes/syc-20377605; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/moles/symptoms-causes/syc-20375200; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/melanoma/symptoms-causes/syc-20374884

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun is the main environmental factor behind skin cancer, premature skin aging and atypical moles. It also triggers or worsens conditions such as cutaneous lupus, rosacea, cold sores, and vitiligo patches, which burn easily. Sun protection is therefore a basic preventive measure in dermatology.

UVB causes sunburn; UVA penetrates deeper and contributes to aging and DNA damage. "Broad-spectrum" sunscreens cover both.

Key recommendations: use broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, applied 15 to 30 minutes before going out and reapplied every two hours and after swimming or sweating, in generous amounts. Avoid direct sun between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., wear UV-protective clothing, a wide-brimmed hat and sunglasses with UV filter, and seek shade. Radiation passes through clouds and reflects off water, sand and snow. Do not use tanning beds.

People who need extra care include children, fair-skinned people, people with many moles or a history of skin cancer, patients with lupus, and those taking photosensitizing drugs such as doxycycline, isotretinoin or some anti-inflammatories. Babies under six months should avoid direct sun and be protected by clothing and shade.

Good sun protection is the most effective way to reduce the risk of skin cancer.
