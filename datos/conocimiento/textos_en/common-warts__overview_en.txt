Common warts: HPV infection, treatment and prevention
Source: https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/hpv-infection/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20351602; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/plantar-warts/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352697; https://medlineplus.gov/spanish/hpv.html; Catálogo clínico del proyecto DermAssist (init/02-catalogo.js)

Warts are benign skin growths caused by human papillomavirus (HPV). Common warts appear on the fingers, hands, elbows and knees; plantar warts on the soles; flat warts on the face and arms. The virus enters through tiny breaks in the skin and spreads by direct contact or by damp surfaces such as locker rooms and pools. Children and teenagers are affected most, and people with weak immunity can have more numerous, persistent warts. Many warts disappear on their own in months to a couple of years.

A common wart is a firm, rough bump, skin-colored or grayish, often with tiny black dots (clotted capillaries). Plantar warts grow inward from pressure and are painful when walking, like a stone in the shoe. They are usually harmless but may bleed if picked.

Treatment is needed if warts hurt, spread or persist. Salicylic acid applied daily for weeks is the standard first step; it should not be used on the face or by people with diabetes or poor circulation without supervision. Cryotherapy with liquid nitrogen, repeated every two to three weeks, is effective. Other options include cantharidin, trichloroacetic acid, imiquimod, laser or surgery. No treatment removes the virus completely, so recurrence can occur.

Prevention: do not pick or bite warts, avoid sharing towels and footwear, wear sandals in public showers, and keep feet dry. See a doctor if a wart bleeds, changes appearance or does not respond, as squamous cell carcinoma can resemble a wart.
