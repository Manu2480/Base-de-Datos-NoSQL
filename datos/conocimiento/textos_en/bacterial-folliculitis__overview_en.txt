Bacterial folliculitis: causes, symptoms, treatment and prevention
Source: https://medlineplus.gov/spanish/ency/article/000823.htm; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es-es/diseases-conditions/folliculitis/symptoms-causes/syc-20361634; Catálogo clínico del proyecto DermAssist (init/02-catalogo.js)

Folliculitis is inflammation of the hair follicles. The bacterial form is most often caused by Staphylococcus aureus entering a damaged follicle. It is common in the beard area, scalp, chest, back, armpits, buttocks and thighs. Shaving, waxing, tight clothing, sweating and moisture favor it, as do diabetes, obesity, acne and long-term use of steroid creams or antibiotics. "Hot tub folliculitis" is caused by Pseudomonas. Deeper forms are boils (furuncles) and carbuncles.

It appears as small red bumps or pustules, each centered on a hair, sometimes itchy or tender, that may drain and crust. Deep infections cause large painful lumps with pus and sometimes fever. Unlike acne, there are no blackheads.

Mild cases often clear on their own in one to two weeks. Warm compresses several times a day, gentle washing and avoiding shaving help. A topical antibiotic such as mupirocin 2% or clindamycin is used for mild infection. Extensive, deep or recurrent infection needs oral antibiotics such as cephalexin or dicloxacillin; MRSA may require trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, doxycycline or clindamycin. Large boils may need drainage by a professional. Never squeeze a boil, especially on the face.

Precautions: with penicillin allergy avoid dicloxacillin and amoxicillin-clavulanate. Doxycycline can cause pseudotumor cerebri and is avoided in pregnancy and with isotretinoin. Patients on methotrexate must not take trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole unsupervised. Do not use steroid creams on infected follicles. Prevent recurrence by using clean razors, not sharing towels, and changing wet clothes promptly.
