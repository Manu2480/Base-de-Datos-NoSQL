Melanocytic nevus (moles): normal features, warning signs and monitoring
Source: https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/moles/symptoms-causes/syc-20375200; https://medlineplus.gov/spanish/melanoma.html

Moles, or melanocytic nevi, are common skin growths made of clusters of melanocytes, the pigment-producing cells. Most people have 10 to 40, appearing in childhood and adolescence. Types include congenital nevi (present at birth; large ones carry a slightly higher cancer risk), acquired nevi (the most common) and dysplastic or atypical nevi, which are larger with irregular borders and uneven color, are not cancer, but mark a higher melanoma risk. Sun exposure, family history and fair skin influence the number of moles. People with more than 50 moles or a family history of melanoma need closer follow-up.

A common mole is round or oval, with regular borders, one uniform color (brown, tan, pink or skin-colored), smaller than about 6 mm. It should not itch, bleed or hurt. Warning signs follow the ABCDE rule: Asymmetry, irregular Borders, multiple Colors, Diameter over 6 mm, and Evolution (change in size, shape, color or symptoms). The "ugly duckling" mole that looks different from the others also deserves attention, as does any new mole in an adult.

Most moles need no treatment. A mole is removed if it is suspicious, irritated by friction or for cosmetic reasons. Surgical excision with pathology is preferred when melanoma is suspected. Never remove moles at home, because this risks infection, scarring and delayed diagnosis.

Prevention and monitoring: use broad-spectrum sunscreen SPF 30 or higher, avoid midday sun and tanning beds, examine your skin monthly and have periodic dermatology checks, with dermoscopy for high-risk people. See a doctor promptly if a mole changes, bleeds or itches.
