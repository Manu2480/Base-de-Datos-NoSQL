Ichthyosis vulgaris: inherited dry, scaly skin and its care
Source: https://medlineplus.gov/spanish/ency/article/001451.htm; https://www.niams.nih.gov/es/informacion-de-salud/Ictiosis

Ichthyosis vulgaris is an inherited disorder of skin keratinization that causes dry, rough skin with fine fish-like scales. It is the most common type of ichthyosis, inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern and linked to mutations in the filaggrin gene, which is needed for the skin barrier. The skin cannot renew itself or retain water normally, so dead cells build up on the surface. It usually begins between three months and five years of age, often worsens in winter and improves in warm, humid climates. It frequently coexists with atopic dermatitis, asthma and keratosis pilaris. It is not contagious and is not due to poor hygiene.

Fine white, gray or brown scales appear on the shins, the outer surfaces of the arms and legs and the back, sparing the skin folds such as the inner elbows, knees and armpits. Palms and soles may show prominent lines. Itching, tightness and painful cracks of the heels and hands are common. Diagnosis is clinical, based on family history, age of onset and distribution; acquired ichthyosis in an adult should prompt a search for underlying disease.

There is no cure, but daily care controls it. Apply thick emollients or ointments twice a day after bathing, use urea (5 to 10%) or lactic acid creams, low-concentration salicylic acid on small areas, and take short lukewarm baths. Topical retinoids can be used in selected cases; oral acitretin is reserved for severe types and is teratogenic and contraindicated in pregnancy. A humidifier in winter and cotton clothing help. Genetic counseling can be useful.
