Epidermolysis bullosa: inherited skin fragility, care and genetics
Source: https://www.niams.nih.gov/es/informacion-de-salud/epidermolisis-ampollosa/basics/symptoms-causes; https://medlineplus.gov/spanish/ency/article/001457.htm; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/diseases-conditions/epidermolysis-bullosa/symptoms-causes/syc-20361062; https://www.msdmanuals.com/es/professional (Manual MSD, epidermólisis ampollosa)

Epidermolysis bullosa (EB) is a group of rare inherited skin diseases in which the skin is so fragile that blisters and wounds form from friction, minor trauma or even without obvious injury. It is caused by mutations in genes for proteins that anchor the layers of the skin together, such as keratins, collagen VII and laminins. It is inherited in an autosomal dominant or recessive pattern depending on the type; with a dominant form an affected parent has a 50% chance of passing it on, and with a recessive form two healthy carrier parents have a 25% chance of an affected child in each pregnancy. It is not contagious. Main types are EB simplex (usually mildest), junctional EB, dystrophic EB (scarring, with deformity of hands and feet) and Kindler syndrome.

Blisters appear on the hands, feet, elbows, knees and areas of friction. In severe forms, blisters affect the mouth, throat and esophagus (difficulty swallowing, strictures), nails, teeth and eyes, and can cause anemia, malnutrition and growth delay. Complications include wound infection and, in recessive dystrophic EB, squamous cell carcinoma from adolescence. Diagnosis uses skin biopsy with immunofluorescence or electron microscopy and genetic testing.

There is no general cure; care is multidisciplinary. Drain large blisters with a sterile needle without removing the roof, use non-adherent silicone or foam dressings, treat infection, control pain and itch, and provide nutritional support. Newer treatments exist, such as a topical wound-healing gel and topical gene therapy for some dystrophic forms.

Home care: handle the baby very gently, use soft clothing without seams, keep the environment cool and avoid ordinary adhesives. Genetic counseling is recommended for families.
