Herpes simplex: oral and genital infection, treatment and complications
Source: https://www.who.int/mediacentre/factsheets/fs400/es/; https://www.quironsalud.com/es/enfermedades-sintomas/herpes-simple

Herpes simplex is a viral infection caused by herpes simplex virus (HSV). HSV-1 mostly causes cold sores around the mouth, while HSV-2 mostly causes genital herpes, although either can infect either site. The virus spreads by direct contact with sores, saliva or genital secretions, and can spread even when no sores are visible. After the first infection it stays dormant in nerve cells for life and can reactivate, triggered by fever, stress, sun exposure, menstruation or weakened immunity.

Many people have no symptoms. When present, tingling or burning comes first, followed by clustered small blisters on a red base that burst, form shallow painful ulcers and heal in 7 to 14 days. The first episode may include fever and swollen glands. Diagnosis is clinical and confirmed by PCR of fluid from a blister.

There is no cure, but antivirals (acyclovir, valacyclovir, famciclovir) shorten outbreaks, ease symptoms and reduce transmission. They work best when started early. People with frequent outbreaks can take daily suppressive therapy. Analgesics and cool compresses help. Avoid kissing and sex during outbreaks, and use condoms.

Complications: eczema herpeticum, widespread herpes on skin with atopic dermatitis, needs urgent antivirals. Eye herpes can damage the cornea and is an emergency. Newborn infection is severe, so pregnant women with genital herpes must tell their doctor. Immunosuppressed patients can have extensive, persistent lesions. Do not apply corticosteroid creams to herpes sores.
